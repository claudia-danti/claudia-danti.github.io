Overview, main results and publications

Research overview

My research asks why planetary systems are so diverse. Around our Sun, no planet grew beyond Earth mass in the inner region, while roughly every other Sun-like star hosts super-Earths (planets between Earth and Neptune in size). I study how this diversity arises from the physics of the protoplanetary disc, the gas and dust disc in which planets form.

My main tool is 1D pebble accretion population synthesis. Pebbles are millimetre to centimetre sized solids that drift inwards through the disc, and planets grow by collecting them. In a population synthesis, many planets are grown in discs with varied initial conditions, so that the simulated planet population can be compared statistically with observed exoplanet demographics (occurrence rates as a function of mass and orbital distance) and with measured planetary compositions.

You can find my code in my GitHub!

Main results

Pebble isolation mass leaves an observable break in planet occurrence (2026)

  • Simulated planets outside the water iceline (the distance beyond which water freezes) do not follow a log-uniform distribution in mass and orbital radius.
  • Once a core reaches the pebble isolation mass (the mass at which it stops accreting pebbles), migration and runaway gas accretion produce a break in occurrence.
  • Between 1 and 50 AU (astronomical units, the Earth-Sun distance), planets of 1 to 5 isolation masses are about 20 times less common than planets of 0.2 to 1 isolation mass.
  • Detecting this break with the Roman microlensing survey would support the core accretion paradigm for giant planet formation.

Super-Earth formation in systems with cold giants (2025)

  • The key uncertainty separating super-Earth systems from terrestrial systems is the degree of viscous heating in the inner disc, which regulates pebble accretion efficiency.
  • Maximally efficient midplane heating suppresses pebble accretion in the terrestrial region, while more realistic heating at higher elevations allows Earth-like embryos to form.
  • The water iceline plays a minor role unless combined with extreme volatile loss and a change in pebble fragmentation velocity.
  • Outer gas giants filter pebbles only weakly unless something delays inner disc growth. This agrees with the lack of a strong super-Earth deficit in observed systems with wide-orbit giants.

Composition of giant planets: pebbles and planetesimals (2023)

  • Atmospheric enrichment is dominated by accretion of gas enriched by inward drifting, evaporating pebbles.
  • Efficient planetesimal formation locks up pebbles and sharply reduces enrichment, so it must be inefficient to explain planets with high heavy-element content.
  • Planetesimal accretion lowers the volatile to refractory ratio, but migration in the inner disc can do the same in the pure pebble case.
  • The two scenarios can be distinguished with atmospheric C/H and O/H ratios (carbon and oxygen relative to hydrogen), which are higher for pure pebble accretion.
